Within their workshop on Gen AI and the effect of it on the act and field of critical making, through a focus on the indie video game industry and coding, Salter and Johnson discussed how the increase in the development of generative AI tools and programs does increase accessibility to game development; however, conversely, due to the simplicity in prompts that lead to complex results, may also make the process and position, at some point in the near future, entirely obsolete.
In the workshop, Salter and Johnson presented two coding methods that can be used to create "choose your own adventure" style, branching path, RPGs (role-playing games). These are text based games which allow the user to select a variety of options within the presented, narrative text. Within the text outlined on the screen, several options are links which the player is encouraged to click. Upon doing so, the player enters onto one of several branching story paths based on the choices (links) selected. These selections continue until an ending is reached, and the player may choose to begin the story again to select a different narrative path and reach a new ending. However, if the developer decides, they can code the story so that, upon seeing select endings, the main story can change slightly to reflect this fact, thus encouraging the player to replay the game a multitude of times and receive different outcomes and events, as the game, seemingly, actively adapts to the player's experience with it. 
	All of the text based games presented in the workshop operated in a similar fashion to that outlined above. In addition, majority had political and social themes and commentary within their narrative, such as those that allow the player to roleplay as a real life multi-millionaire or billionaire. Upon being given a set amount, the player selected options presented to determine how to spend their funds. Upon doing so, the consequences of their actions (both positive and negative) were described and the player could choose another option to react accordingly. Aside from providing an entertaining experience, these games also encouraged contemplation and consideration about societal issues and spoke out against problematic norms in a unique and engaging way. These games were also extremely accessible, being provided at a low cost if any and posted on itch.io, an open source website in which indie (independent) developers can post their content.
	Upon providing a demonstration of these text based games and showcasing their proliferation and platforms on which they are posted, Salter and Johnson moved into their primary inquiry, that is, what is, or could be, the potential impact of generative AI on the development of video games, especially those created by smaller indie developers (such as those presented in the workshop). During this segment, they asked the audience for a prompt and entered it into the Gen AI, Claude. This, they shared, was a new model only recently introduced but with capabilities leagues above what had previously existed. This model was tasked with a simple, non-descriptive, prompt: to create a retro style RPG, browser game about avoiding sea monsters based on a full e-mail inbox. Within roughly twenty minutes or so, the model was able to create a game with graphics, a soundtrack, and more. This was, of course, based on assets on which it was trained and used to generate this particular product. Such a development, per Salter and Johnson, may put developers (in professional settings) and similar positions at risk for dissolution in the future.
